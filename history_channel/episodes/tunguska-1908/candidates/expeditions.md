# expeditions — VI. The Search Goes On

Kulik returned again and again. In 1928 and 1929, with larger teams, he mapped the fallen forest and photographed the damage. Those photographs are the ones that made Tunguska famous around the world. In 1938 he arranged an aerial survey, and fifteen hundred photographs of the flattened forest were taken from the air. Decades later, in 1975, the original negatives were burned on official orders, because the old nitrate film was a fire hazard. Fortunately, prints had been kept in the city of Tomsk.
