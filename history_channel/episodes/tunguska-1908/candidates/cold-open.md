# cold-open — 

On a summer morning in 1908, the sky above Siberia exploded. Millions of trees were knocked flat over an area larger than the whole of Greater London, all pointing away from a single spot. A man sixty kilometres away felt as if his shirt was on fire. Barometers in Britain twitched. For nights afterwards, Europeans could read the newspaper outdoors at midnight. When a scientist finally reached the site, nineteen years later, he found no crater at all, only a forest of standing, burnt, branchless trees. What happened at Tunguska, and could it happen again?
