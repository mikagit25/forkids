# canal — VIII. The Canal

Without yellow fever, the canal could be built. Tens of thousands of workers dug through mountains, built enormous locks and dammed a river to create a lake. On the fifteenth of August 1914, the steamship Ancon made the first official passage, and the Panama Canal opened, linking the Atlantic and the Pacific. The First World War had begun only days before, and the grand celebrations that had been planned were cancelled. It was one of the greatest engineering projects in history. And it was, in part, a victory of medicine over a mosquito.
