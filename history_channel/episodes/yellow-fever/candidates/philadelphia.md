# philadelphia — II. Philadelphia, 1793

In the summer of 1793, Philadelphia was the capital of the United States and its largest city. That August, yellow fever broke out near the docks. Within weeks it was everywhere. Around twenty thousand people fled the city, including President George Washington and much of the federal government. Those who stayed watched carts carry the dead through empty streets. People walked with handkerchiefs soaked in vinegar pressed to their noses. In the worst week, in early October, seven hundred and eleven deaths were reported.
