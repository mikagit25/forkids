# vaccine — IX. The Vaccine

Mosquito control worked in cities, but yellow fever also lived in monkeys in the jungles of Africa and South America, so it could never be wiped out that way. In 1927, scientists isolated the yellow fever virus in West Africa. Ten years later, in 1937, the South African virologist Max Theiler, working at the Rockefeller Foundation in New York, developed a vaccine called 17D. With his colleague Hugh Smith, he weakened the virus by growing it again and again, more than a hundred times, in chicken embryos, until it no longer caused disease.
