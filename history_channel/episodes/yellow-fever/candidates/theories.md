# theories — IV. Filth, Fomites and a Cuban Doctor

Through the nineteenth century, yellow fever struck again and again, from New Orleans to Rio de Janeiro. At least twenty-five major outbreaks struck the Americas in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, and New Orleans was hit again and again, worst of all in 1833 and 1853. Most doctors believed it spread through filth and miasma, or through the clothes and bedding of the sick. Cities burned tar in the streets and fumigated houses.
