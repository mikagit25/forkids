# closing — Epilogue

The 1918 pandemic killed more people than the war it travelled with. Yet for decades it was almost forgotten, overshadowed by the armistice and the peace. Its lessons were written in the records of crowded hospitals, emergency ordinances and mass graves. In Philadelphia and St. Louis. In Western and American Samoa. And they came back into focus every time a new virus began to spread. The children who sang about a little bird named Enza grew up, and many of them never forgot why.
