# world — IX. Around the World

No country suffered more than India. The virus is thought to have arrived in Bombay in the summer of 1918, perhaps on a troop ship returning from Europe. It then followed the railways inland, through towns and villages already weakened by a failed monsoon and food shortages. Historians estimate that at least twelve million Indians died, and possibly far more. That may be a quarter or more of all the deaths in the world. In Brazil, the virus did not spare the powerful.
