# cold-open — 

It sounds like a joke. A flood of molasses, sweeping through the streets of an American city. But on a January day in 1919, a giant tank in Boston split open, and a wave of syrup taller than a house tore through a crowded neighbourhood. It knocked buildings off their foundations, bent the steel of an elevated railway and swallowed people, horses and a fire station. Twenty-one people died. The company blamed anarchists. The families took it to court. And the verdict changed the way buildings are designed across America. This is the story of the Great Molasses Flood.
