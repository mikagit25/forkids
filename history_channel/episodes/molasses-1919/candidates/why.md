# why — II. Why Molasses?

Molasses had been part of New England's trade for two centuries. Ships carried it north from the sugar plantations of the Caribbean, where it was distilled into rum — part of the same Atlantic trade that carried enslaved Africans across the ocean. But by 1919, molasses was not just for baking or rum. It could be fermented and distilled into industrial alcohol, a key ingredient in making munitions. During the First World War, demand had soared.
