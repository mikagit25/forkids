# north-end — I. The North End

Boston's North End was one of the most crowded neighbourhoods in America. Wave after wave of newcomers had settled here: first the Irish, then Jewish families from Eastern Europe, and then Italians. By 1910, Italian immigrants alone made up a quarter of all its residents, packed into tenements near the harbour. Along Commercial Street ran the docks, warehouses, a fire station and the Atlantic Avenue elevated railway, opened in 1901, carrying trains above the street on steel girders.
