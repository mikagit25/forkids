# plan — I. The Last Great Journey

By 1914, the South Pole had already been reached, first by Roald Amundsen, then by Robert Falcon Scott, who died on the way back. Ernest Shackleton, an Anglo-Irish explorer who had come within a hundred miles of the Pole a few years earlier, wanted the one prize left: to cross the entire continent on foot, from sea to sea. His plan needed two ships. The Endurance would carry his party to the Weddell Sea.
