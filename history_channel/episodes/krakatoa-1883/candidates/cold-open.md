# cold-open — 

At two minutes past ten on the morning of the twenty-seventh of August 1883, a volcano in the Dutch East Indies made the loudest sound in recorded history. It was heard in Australia, more than three thousand kilometres away. On a small island in the Indian Ocean, nearly five thousand kilometres away, people thought it was the guns of a ship in distress. The pressure wave went around the whole Earth three and a half times. Waves more than thirty metres high swept away towns and villages. At least thirty-six thousand people died.
