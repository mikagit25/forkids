# race — IX. The Race for a Vaccine

The first attempts at a vaccine ended badly. In 1935, a young researcher in New York, Maurice Brodie, made a vaccine from virus grown in monkey spinal cords and killed with formaldehyde. He tested it on himself and his assistants, and then on three thousand children. Many had allergic reactions, and none of them became immune. The breakthrough came in Boston at the end of the 1940s. John Enders, Thomas Weller and Frederick Robbins found a way to grow the poliovirus in human tissue in the laboratory, instead of in live monkeys.
