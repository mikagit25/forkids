# paradox — II. A Disease of Clean Cities

Here is the strange thing. For thousands of years polio was a quiet, scattered disease. Large epidemics began only around 1900, in the richest countries in the world. The first report of several cases together came from Louisiana, in 1841. In 1894, Vermont had the first recognised epidemic in the United States: a hundred and thirty-two cases and eighteen deaths. By 1907, New York City reported about two and a half thousand cases. The explanation, as scientists later understood it, was cleanliness. In earlier centuries, almost every child met the virus very early, before the age of four. [...]
