# century — VII. Still a Killer

Even presidents were not safe. In November 1863, Abraham Lincoln was already falling ill when he delivered the Gettysburg Address. Days later, his doctors found that he had smallpox. Two decades later, in Georgia, a small boy named Joseph Stalin caught smallpox, and carried its scars on his face for the rest of his life. Despite vaccination, smallpox remained one of the great killers of the twentieth century. Vaccination was uneven, especially in poorer countries, and outbreaks continued around the world.
