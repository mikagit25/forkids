# variolation — III. Fighting Fire with Fire

Long before anyone understood viruses, people noticed that survivors of smallpox never caught it again. In China, India, the Ottoman Empire and parts of Africa, healers practised variolation: taking material from a smallpox sore and introducing it into a small cut on a healthy person, or blowing powdered scabs into the nose. Most people developed a milder case, and were protected for life. In 1721, Lady Mary Wortley Montagu, who had seen variolation in Constantinople and survived smallpox herself, had her daughter variolated in London.
