# jenner — IV. The Milkmaid's Secret

In the English countryside, there was an old belief that milkmaids who caught cowpox, a mild disease from cows' udders, did not catch smallpox. A country doctor in Gloucestershire, Edward Jenner, decided to test it. On the fourteenth of May 1796, he took material from a cowpox sore on the hand of a milkmaid named Sarah Nelmes, and inoculated James Phipps, the eight-year-old son of his gardener. Sarah had caught cowpox from a cow named Blossom, whose hide still hangs on the wall of the library at St George's medical school in London. Weeks later, Jenner exposed the boy to smallpox.
