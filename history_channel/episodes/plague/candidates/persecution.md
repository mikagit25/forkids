# persecution — V. Scapegoats

Fear turned to hatred. Across Europe, Jewish communities were falsely accused of poisoning wells to spread the disease. Pope Clement the Sixth issued two papal bulls in 1348 condemning the accusations. His own physician, Guy de Chauliac, who had treated plague victims himself, argued that Jews were not to blame, and that they were dying of the plague like everyone else. It made little difference. On the fourteenth of February 1349, in Strasbourg, around two thousand Jews were burned alive, before the plague had even reached the city.
