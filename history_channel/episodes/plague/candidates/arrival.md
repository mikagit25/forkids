# arrival — III. The Great Mortality

In October 1347, the plague reached Sicily. From there it spread with terrifying speed: to Italy, France, Spain and England by the summer of 1348, then Germany, Scandinavia and finally Russia. In England, the first known case was a sailor who landed at Weymouth, in Dorset, in June 1348. By the autumn it was in London, and by the following summer it covered the whole country. Most victims developed the bubonic form. A high fever was followed by painful swellings, called buboes, in the groin, armpits or neck. Many died within days.
