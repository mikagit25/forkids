# origin — I. Out of Asia

The plague is caused by a bacterium, Yersinia pestis. It lives in wild rodents and is spread among them by fleas. When infected rodents die, their hungry fleas look for new hosts, and sometimes those hosts are human. Ancient DNA has shown that plague infected people thousands of years ago, and one great pandemic, the Plague of Justinian, struck the Mediterranean in the sixth century. It appeared in 541 at the Egyptian port of Pelusium, and reached Constantinople the following year.
