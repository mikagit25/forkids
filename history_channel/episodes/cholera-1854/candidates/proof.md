# proof — XI. Proof

In 1866, cholera struck London one last time. More than five and a half thousand people died. This time the outbreak was confined almost entirely to one part of the East End, between Aldgate and Bow, one of the few areas not yet connected to Bazalgette's system. Its water came from a company that had been releasing contaminated water into the supply. The pattern was impossible to explain with bad air. Even William Farr, the government statistician and one of Snow's chief opponents, now saw it. He ordered that no unboiled water should be drunk. It was exactly what John Snow had predicted. [...]
