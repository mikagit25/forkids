# sewers — X. Bazalgette's Sewers

Parliament acts quickly when it can smell the problem. In August 1858, it passed a law giving the city the money and power to fix it. The Metropolitan Board of Works could borrow three million pounds, to be repaid by a small charge on every London household for the next forty years. The job went to the engineer Joseph Bazalgette. His plan was enormous: about eighty-two miles of huge brick sewers, fed by around eleven hundred miles of street sewers, carrying waste east, away from the city, before releasing it downstream.
