# stink — IX. The Great Stink

In June 1858, London baked in a heatwave. The level of the Thames dropped, and the sewage that normally flowed away was left rotting on its banks in the sun. The smell became unbearable. It was so bad that inside the Houses of Parliament, beside the river, curtains were soaked in chloride of lime in an attempt to block it out. Members even discussed moving Parliament out of London. A newspaper reported that Benjamin Disraeli, the future prime minister, hurried out of a committee room with a mass of papers in one hand and his handkerchief pressed to his nose.
