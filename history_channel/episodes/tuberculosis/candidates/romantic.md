# romantic — III. The Romantic Disease

Strangely, the nineteenth century also romanticised consumption. Because it often struck young people, and slowly made them thin, pale and flushed, it became associated with sensitivity, beauty and artistic genius. Some fashionable women even tried to look consumptive. The disease took a long list of artists and writers. The composer Frédéric Chopin suffered from chronic lung disease for years, almost certainly tuberculosis, and his letters and the writings of George Sand describe his coughing fits.
