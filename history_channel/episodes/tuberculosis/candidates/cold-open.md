# cold-open — 

It has been found in human bones nine thousand years old. In the nineteenth century, it killed about a quarter of Europe's adults. Poets and composers died of it, and people thought it made them beautiful. Doctors prescribed mountain air, rest, and sleeping outdoors in the snow. Whole towns were built around patients waiting to get better, or to die. Then, in 1944, a young American scientist found the first drug that could cure it. And yet today, tuberculosis still kills more people than any other single infection on Earth. This is the biography of the white plague.
