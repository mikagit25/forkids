# cure — VIII. The First Cure

The first weapon was a vaccine. In France, Albert Calmette and Camille Guérin spent years weakening a strain of cattle tuberculosis. Their BCG vaccine was first given to a human in 1921, and is still used today, especially to protect children. But the real breakthrough came in 1944, at Rutgers University, in New Jersey. A young graduate student, Albert Schatz, working in the laboratory of Selman Waksman, isolated a new antibiotic from soil bacteria: streptomycin. It killed the tuberculosis bacterium.
