# white-plague — II. The White Plague

In the crowded cities of the Industrial Revolution, tuberculosis exploded. Workers lived packed into damp, airless rooms, and the bacterium passed easily from one to the next. By the nineteenth century, it was killing about a quarter of Europe's adult population. People called it the white plague, after the deathly pallor of its victims. Its symptoms were slow and cruel: a persistent cough, fevers and night sweats, weight loss, and finally coughing up blood. Some patients lived with it for years, slowly fading.
