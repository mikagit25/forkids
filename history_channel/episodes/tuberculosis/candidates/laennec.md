# laennec — IV. Listening to the Chest

Diagnosis was difficult, until a French doctor named René Laennec had an idea. In 1816, examining a young woman, he felt it would be improper to put his ear directly to her chest. He rolled a sheet of paper into a tube, placed one end on her chest and the other to his ear, and was amazed at how clearly he could hear her heart. He developed this into a wooden instrument, the stethoscope, and used it to describe the sounds of diseased lungs in remarkable detail. Laennec himself died of tuberculosis, at the age of forty-five.
