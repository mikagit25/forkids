wanted: up to 3 photos

> In the crowded cities of the Industrial Revolution, tuberculosis exploded. Workers lived packed into damp, airless rooms, and the bacterium passed easily from one to the next. By the nineteenth century, it was killing about a quarter of Europe's adult population. People called it the white plague, after the deathly pallor of its victims. Its symptoms were slow and cruel: a persistent cough, fevers and night sweats, weight loss, and finally coughing up blood. Some patients lived with it for years, slowly fading.

## scene `koch` — V. Koch's Discovery 24 March 1882
wanted: up to 3 photos
searches: Robert Koch laboratory, Robert Koch, tubercle bacillus Koch drawing

> For centuries, many doctors believed tuberculosis was hereditary, or caused by a weak constitution. On the twenty-fourth of March 1882, in Berlin, the German physician Robert Koch stood before the Physiological Society and announced that he had found its cause: a bacterium, which he had managed to stain and see under the microscope, and to grow in the laboratory. It was a turning point in medicine. Tuberculosis was infectious. It could be prevented. Today, the twenty-fourth of March is World Tuberculosis Day.
